# Adult Position & Motion Prompt Library

This reference is a reusable choreography library for explicit fictional-adult sexual scenes authored through the parent H3 tailchain skill. It is not loaded for nonsexual scenes. All depicted characters must be adults.

The purpose of this file is to make recurring adult actions reproducible, anatomically coherent, high-impact when requested, and compatible with Picture 3 tail-lineart continuation. It contains reusable motion geometry and prompt language, not fixed characters, locations, clothing, dialogue, climax timing, or camera style. User constraints and the active scene always take precedence.

## 1. Routing

Use this reference when the scene includes penetrative intercourse, oral sex, manual genital stimulation, an explicit position transition, broad delegated adult choreography, or a request for stronger/rougher/higher-cadence/larger-amplitude sexual motion.

Do not paste every template into every segment. Select only the template matching the current action and rewrite it around the current subjects, support points, camera axis, intensity, and inherited Picture 3 phase.

This catalog is **not a whitelist**. Real AV choreography can use positions or hybrids not listed below. When a scene calls for a library-external position, first derive it from physical constraints instead of inventing only a name. Define: (1) every support point, (2) both pelvis/body orientations, (3) the driving subject and force path, (4) the anatomical contact axis, (5) the maximum physically available travel, (6) how the receiving body absorbs/rebounds, (7) how the female visual anchor remains readable, and (8) whether contact is preserved, released, or re-established during entry into the pose. Once that geometry is coherent, the new position is valid for the current prompt. If it is broadly reusable, add it as a new template to this file.

For high-intensity cyclic motion, combine the selected position template with v18-motion-intensity.md. For anatomy/contact continuity, also follow adult-action-continuity.md.

## 2. Shared High-Impact Sexual Motion Grammar

### 2.1 Full-cycle structure

A repeated cycle should visibly contain:

1. load / withdrawal — enough travel to make the next return legible;
2. decisive return — the driving body accelerates along the established anatomical axis;
3. compression / contact peak — both bodies visibly absorb the force through pelvis, torso, bedding, wall, knees, elbows, hands, or other established supports;
4. elastic rebound — compression releases immediately into the next cycle instead of pausing;
5. continuous phase — the next repetition starts from the actual ending phase rather than resetting to a preparation pose.

At normal real-time 1×, high cadence comes from denser physical cycles, not fast-forward, speed ramps, frame skipping, or tiny shallow oscillation.

Reusable execution fragment:

> Use long-travel, impact-driven reciprocal motion at normal real-time 1×. Each cycle shows a clearly visible loading/withdrawal phase, decisive acceleration through the return, a brief compression/contact peak, and immediate elastic rebound into the next cycle. Sustain a high physical cadence with almost no dead pause while preserving one stable anatomical axis. Prefer large usable travel on every cycle; do not collapse the action into shallow rapid micro-movements merely to look fast.

### 2.2 Whole-body driving chain

Do not isolate the action to a tiny pelvic twitch unless the user explicitly requests small motion. The driver recruits the body regions physically available in the current position: hips/pelvis for the main reciprocal path, thighs and knees for travel, feet/calves for standing support and push-off, trunk/abdomen for force transfer, and arms/shoulders for stabilization.

Reusable execution fragment:

> The driving performer generates the motion through the hips, thighs and trunk as one connected kinetic chain rather than isolated pelvic pulses. Supporting limbs flex and re-extend naturally to create and absorb the travel.

### 2.3 Receiving-body response

High-impact motion should propagate through the receiving body instead of leaving it visually frozen. Depending on the position, show pelvis translation, abdomen/torso compression and release, shoulder or upper-back recoil, support-arm/knee flexion, mattress/cushion compression, and delayed secondary inertia in hair, breasts, buttocks, thighs or other visible soft tissue.

Secondary motion is a consequence of the primary action. It must not become a separate mechanical loop.

Reusable execution fragment:

> The force visibly propagates through the receiving body: the pelvis shifts along the established axis, the abdomen and torso compress and release, support points flex and recover, and visible soft tissue and hair show natural delayed secondary inertia. The receiving body must not remain unnaturally locked while the driving body moves.

## 3. Supine Face-to-Face / Missionary

Geometry:
- receiving adult lies on the back;
- penetrating adult faces the receiver;
- pelvises remain aligned front-to-front;
- receiver's back/shoulders remain supported by the bed or floor;
- penetrating adult supports weight through knees/feet plus forearms/hands as established;
- penetrating anatomy remains continuously attached to the penetrating pelvis.

Reusable execution fragment:

> In the established supine face-to-face position, <Subject 2> drives each cycle through a broad backward pelvic load followed by a decisive forward return. His thighs and trunk contribute to the travel while his established supports absorb and release the force. <Subject 1>'s pelvis translates against the bedding, her abdomen and torso compress and rebound, and her shoulders, hair and breasts show delayed secondary inertia. The penis remains continuously attached to <Subject 2>'s pelvis and penetration stays on one coherent pelvis-to-pelvis axis throughout.

Camera: prefer side-front, three-quarter, or over-shoulder views when the female face is the visual anchor. Keep enough pelvis/torso context to make ownership and axis readable.

## 4. Supine Legs Raised / Legs on Shoulders

Geometry:

- penetrating adult remains aligned between the legs;
- receiver's shoulders/back remain the stable base;
- raised legs do not teleport between shoulder/arm positions across cuts.

Camera: a male-rear/over-shoulder view aimed toward the receiver can keep her face visible while preserving both pelvises and the raised-leg geometry.

## 16. Position Transitions

Do not switch between templates by teleporting at a cut.

### 16.1 Contact preserved

Use only when the body rotation/repositioning is physically compatible with continuous intimate contact.

> Maintain the existing intimate contact while the subjects shift weight through the established hands/knees/feet, rotate their pelvises along one continuous path, and settle into the new support geometry. The next cyclic movement begins from the resulting pose; the transition is not replayed.

### 16.2 Release then re-establish

Preferred when keeping penetration through the transition would require impossible geometry.

> The existing penetration visibly releases first. The subjects then move through one continuous weight-transfer path into the new support geometry. After both pelvises are aligned in the new position, the requested penetration is re-established once. Subsequent motion continues from that state without replaying either the withdrawal or the setup.

### 16.3 Non-penetrative bridge

Use kissing, body contact, manual/oral action, or a brief repositioning only when it belongs to the authorized choreography.

## 17. Picture 3 Seam Rules for Cyclic Sexual Motion

For Segment 2+ in the tail-lineart workflow:

- Picture 3 owns the opening pose, support geometry, camera axis, contact points and visible phase of the current cycle;
- do not restart from about-to-enter, begins-riding, moves-into-position, or another setup beat if Picture 3 already shows the action underway;
- continue from the inherited direction: if the tail is near the loaded/withdrawn phase, complete the return; if it is near compression/contact peak, rebound into the next load;
- maintain the same driver/receiver roles and anatomical ownership across the seam;
- text carries nudity, colors, materials, lighting and other non-geometric mutable state;

Reusable continuation fragment:

> Opening Geometry follows <Picture 3> for pose, supports, pelvis orientation, contact points, camera axis and the visible phase of the ongoing cycle. Continue forward from that exact phase: complete the current travel direction, rebound naturally, and sustain the established cadence without replaying setup or initial penetration.

## 18. Female Visual-Anchor Camera Patterns

### 18.1 Default priority

In two-person adult scenes, default to **see the woman's face first**. The male performer's frontal face is optional unless the user explicitly requests it. Composition, occlusion, head angle, and camera placement should be solved primarily in favor of preserving the woman's frontal or three-quarter face.

When a framing tradeoff is necessary:
- preserve the woman's eyes, nose bridge, cheeks, jawline, and facial readability first;
- allow the man to be shown from behind, over-shoulder, partial side profile, cheek/jaw edge, neck, or back-of-head;
- if one performer must be partially occluded, prefer occluding the man;
- prefer the woman's face around frontal to roughly 20–35 degrees three-quarter rather than a full side profile;
- keep enough torso/pelvis/support geometry to read the active action.

### 18.2 Female-face over-shoulder template

Use this pattern whenever the woman is the visual anchor and the action is face-to-face, including kissing, wall sex, missionary, seated straddle, and similar frontal pair positions.

- camera sits just behind and slightly above the man's shoulder;
- the man occupies only a limited foreground edge with shoulder / cheek edge / back of head;
- the woman's face remains dominant in frame;
- the woman's face stays frontal-to-three-quarter, ideally around 20–35 degrees off axis;
- do not drift into a full side-profile two-shot unless the user explicitly asks for it;
- if kissing would normally force both subjects into profile, rotate or offset the man's head first rather than sacrificing the woman's facial readability;
- preserve enough torso / pelvis context to keep the action anatomically readable.

Reusable execution fragment:

> The camera stays just behind and slightly above <Subject 2>'s shoulder, looking toward <Subject 1>. <Subject 2>'s shoulder, cheek edge, and back of head remain only a limited foreground frame element and must not block <Subject 1>'s eyes, nose bridge, cheeks, or jawline. <Subject 1> remains the dominant facial subject in a frontal-to-three-quarter view, approximately 20–35 degrees off axis. If the intimate contact becomes tighter, adjust <Subject 2>'s head angle and foreground placement rather than letting the shot collapse into a side-profile two-shot.

Priority rule:

> If a framing choice must be made, preserve <Subject 1>'s facial readability first; <Subject 2>'s frontal face is optional unless explicitly requested by the user.

### 18.3 Position-specific camera patterns

When the woman is the camera-priority subject, select an angle that preserves her face and enough action context:

- supine: male-rear/over-shoulder slightly high, side-front three-quarter, or low front three-quarter toward her face;
- kneeling rear entry: side-front toward her face, front three-quarter with hips still readable, or a controlled orbit that never loses the body axis;
- woman-on-top: lower-partner POV-like, low front three-quarter, or side-front medium shot;
- seated face-to-face: partner shoulder/three-quarter framing with her face unobstructed;
- standing: side-front or partner-shoulder view that includes the support surface and pelvis alignment.

Do not solve face visibility by cutting to a detached face-only portrait during explicit cyclic action. Keep a recognizable shoulder line, partner body edge, support point, or pelvis/torso context in frame.

## 19. AV Kissing Standard — Open-Mouth French Kissing

Unless the user explicitly requests a soft, closed-mouth, romantic, teasing, or restrained kiss, adult AV choreography defaults to an intense open-mouth French kiss rather than a light peck.

Physical components:

- both adults clearly part their lips before contact;
- mouths press together with changing pressure instead of a fixed closed-mouth pose;
- tongues actively meet, slide and re-engage inside the shared mouth space;
- heads change angle naturally left/right so noses and jaws do not collide;
- jaws and necks remain visibly active rather than frozen;
- the pair may briefly break mouth contact for breath, leaving lips close, then immediately re-engage;
- hands may stabilize on jaw, neck, shoulder, back, waist, hip or another already authorized body landmark;
- if kissing occurs during intercourse, riding or another cyclic action, the pelvis/body cadence continues underneath the kiss rather than pausing;
- female moans/gasps become muffled during full mouth contact and become clearer again during brief releases.

Reusable execution fragment:

> They crash into an intense open-mouth French kiss with both mouths visibly open and their tongues actively meeting and sliding against each other. Their heads keep changing angle as their jaws and necks move with the kiss; they press faces close, break apart only for brief irregular breaths, then immediately re-engage. The ongoing body action continues at the established cadence underneath the kiss instead of stopping for the kiss.

Camera: prefer a close or medium-close two-shot/over-shoulder angle where both mouths, jaw movement, at least one eye/cheek line of the visual-anchor woman, and some continuing body-action context remain readable. Avoid a face-only detached portrait if a simultaneous explicit action must remain legible.

## 20. Vocal and Expression Overlay

Motion and vocal reaction are separate systems. Do not map one moan to every cycle.

When vocal reactions are authorized:
- stronger return/contact peaks may trigger occasional sharper gasps or fuller moans;
- sustained high cadence may make reactions somewhat more frequent while retaining irregular spacing;
- mouth obstruction from kissing/oral action muffles sound until the mouth is free;
- brief eye closure, jaw release, brow tension, head movement, grip tightening and torso tension vary with the current physical trigger;
- a camera glance must not pause the ongoing action.

Use h3-sound-prompt-standard.md for the full two-layer sound contract.
